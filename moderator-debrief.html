KENEFLEX

Participant Debrief

Moderator-led formative research. Protect the order: spontaneous Keneflex reaction first, comparison second, brand-promise audit third, founder-intent reveal last.

Research hygiene: use P1–P6 rather than names. Capture verbatim phrases whenever possible. Do not explain, defend, or pitch Keneflex until the Founder-Intent section.

1. Keneflex-only debrief

Ask immediately after the participant reaches the prototype stopping point. Do not mention ChatGPT, How It Works, Our Approach, or the founder intention yet.
What would you do next?
Ask exactly as written. Do not offer choices.
Before acting, would you check anywhere else? Where and why?

Do not suggest research, comparison, sizing, safety, or curation.

3. Strong ChatGPT comparison

Show the prepared comparator only after Section 1 is locked. Give the participant time to read it before asking these questions.
Which experience leaves you more ready to act, and why?
Which required less work from you? What work was different?
Which felt more specific? Why?
Which felt more complete? What made it feel complete or incomplete?
What, if anything, would you get from Keneflex that you would not get by simply using ChatGPT?
What did ChatGPT do better?
If both were available right now at no extra cost, which would you use next time?

4. Promise vs. experience audit

Now ask the participant to open/read “How it works” and “Our approach.” They have already given the clean comparison answers, so this becomes a truth-test of our stated promise.
Does this description match what you just experienced?
What feels accurate?
What feels exaggerated, unsupported, or missing from the actual experience?
Is anything important about what Keneflex actually did missing from these explanations?

5. Founder-intent reveal

This is the first time the moderator explicitly explains the intended company. Read this neutrally—do not sell it.
“What we are trying to build is not just a site that recommends products. The goal is for someone to come in with a confusing over-the-counter health problem, use their own words, and have Keneflex do the work of narrowing what matters, comparing the relevant options, building the right level of solution, explaining the reasoning, adapting to things like products they already have, home remedies, preferences or budget, and helping them feel the decision is finished rather than needing to keep researching.”
Knowing that is the goal, where did this experience hit the intention well?
Where did it miss the point?
What part of that intention did you not feel as a user?
What single change would most improve the gap?
If Keneflex wants to become the first place you would go for an OTC health-product problem, what is still missing?
